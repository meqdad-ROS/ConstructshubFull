# Book types: what changes between them

The craft principles apply to all three; this file covers what differs.

## Undergraduate textbook
- **Reader**: student meeting the subject for the first time; instructor choosing a text.
- **Structure**: chapter pattern in SKILL.md in full; objectives, many worked examples (typically 4–8 per chapter), summaries, 20–40 graded problems per chapter, answers to selected problems in an appendix, a separate solutions manual for instructors.
- **Pace**: small steps, frequent checks, running example, "in practice" boxes linking to labs and projects.
- **Extras**: course-path table in the preface; chapter prerequisites; optional sections marked with a symbol (e.g. $\star$) for advanced material; MATLAB/Python snippets where computation matters.

## Graduate / research monograph
- **Reader**: graduate students, researchers, advanced practitioners.
- **Structure**: fewer, deeper chapters; each chapter opens by locating its contribution relative to the literature and closes with bibliographic notes discussing competing approaches and open problems.
- **Style**: theorem–proof or proposition–derivation where appropriate; rigorous assumptions; comparative tables of methods; the author's own research framed carefully and honestly (clearly separate established results from the author's proposals).
- **Problems**: fewer but harder; research-style exercises and projects.
- **Citations**: dense and accurate; each chapter's References list is longer (often 30+ entries) and weighted toward recent peer-reviewed papers.

## Professional handbook / reference
- **Reader**: practising engineer looking something up under time pressure.
- **Structure**: modular sections that stand alone; numbered procedures; design tables, rules of thumb with their validity limits, checklists, worked design calculations to standards.
- **Style**: terse, imperative, no long derivations (move them to an appendix); every number traceable to a standard or code named in the text (edition/year), or to a book or paper in the chapter's References.
- **Safety**: flag where local codes (e.g. national building or electrical codes) override general guidance; never present a design value as authoritative without its source.

## Book proposal (often needed before any of the above)
Publishers typically expect: working title; one-paragraph pitch; target audience and courses; competing titles and how this book differs; detailed TOC with chapter summaries; sample chapter; author credentials; length, figure count, and timeline. Offer to draft this when the user is planning a new book.
